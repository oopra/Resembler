Resembler

Whose eyes? Whose nose? Whose stubborn chin? Put the photos side by side and find out — feature by feature.

Runs entirely on your device. No account, no upload, no key. Works offline once loaded.

1 The little one

One clear, straight-on photo, looking at the camera with a relaxed face. Big in the frame beats far away; a neutral expression beats a big grin, because a grin genuinely moves the mouth.

2 Who to compare against

Mum, Dad, a grandparent, an uncle — up to six.

3 Compare

How carefully?

Each reading measures the same faces from a slightly different crop. Finding a face is never pixel-perfect, so anything that shifts between readings is measurement noise — and where that noise is bigger than the difference between people, the result says so instead of picking a winner.

✦ The verdict

Feature by feature

For fun, and only for fun. This measures what two faces look like in two photographs. It is not a paternity test, not a DNA test and not face recognition — it cannot tell you who is related to whom, and a different pair of photos of the same two people can give a different answer. Children also change enormously as they grow; the toddler who is “all Dad” is frequently a teenager who is all Mum.

How it works. A face-mesh model runs in this page and marks 478 points on each face. Those become about thirty measurements — the gap between the eyes, the width of the nose against its length, the angle of the jaw, the balance of the lips — each taken relative to the distance between the pupils, so distance from the camera and the difference between a child’s head and an adult’s largely cancel out. Similar measurements score high; the scores are grouped into features and weighted towards bone structure, which is what survives twenty years of growing.

What it will not measure. Ears and hairline: a face mesh stops at the face, so those are left out rather than guessed at. Anything an expression moves — a smile widening the mouth, a raised brow — is dropped from the comparison when either photo is pulling that face, and the result tells you it was dropped. A face turned or tipped away from the camera is flagged rather than measured, because turning a head foreshortens one side of everything.

Where your photos go: nowhere. Everything happens in this page. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and there is no server to store it on — the model and its runtime are files served alongside this page, about 16 MB the first time, cached afterwards so it works with the network off.
